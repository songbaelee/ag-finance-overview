Aceli's actual vs. projected loan volume

Aceli's pre-launch plan assumed international impact investors would supply roughly half of loan volume, with a small number of African-domiciled lenders (target: 10) making up the rest. In practice, 45 African-domiciled lenders drove 96% of the 5,576 loans made — local banks had branch networks reaching smaller, more remote agri-SMEs in a way international funds, which typically serve several countries from a single regional office and favor larger loan sizes for their own operating economics, could not. Aceli also lowered its own minimum loan size over time (from $25k to $15k to $10k) to intentionally reach smaller, higher-additionality borrowers — a deliberate policy choice, not a miss, made in exchange for lower capital leverage. The takeaway: loan-size distribution in practice is driven by who ends up lending, not just top-down assumptions about market demand — channel choice can overwhelm even a well-informed volume/size projection, which matters for anyone designing a similar facility's own targets.
